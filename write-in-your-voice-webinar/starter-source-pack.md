# Fictional starter source pack

Use these examples if your own source material is not safe to share during the
workshop. They are fictional and contain no customer, company, or personal
data.

## Source 01 — short proposal

### Proposal: one-week launch-message review

Northline Studio is preparing to explain a new service. The team has plenty of
ideas but no agreed first message. They do not need a brand strategy document.
They need a short, usable starting point that gives them something concrete to
react to.

In one week, I will read the supplied notes and three relevant customer
conversations; name the strongest message, intended audience, and deliberate
omissions; draft one homepage section and three short posts; then separate
supported claims from assumptions in a review.

The result is one message brief, one homepage draft, three post drafts, and a
short list of what to keep, change, or test. Nothing is published or sent. A
claim without support is marked as a question rather than certainty.

## Source 02 — short newsletter post

### A useful draft is not a finished thought

The most useful thing an AI draft can do is give you something specific to
disagree with.

A real first version has a point of view, a boundary, and a question you can
answer. Give it a few examples. Tell it what is out of bounds. Ask it to show
what it used and what it could not know. Then correct one thing in public view.

The correction is the part worth keeping. After three honest runs, you have the
beginning of a way of working.

## Source 03 — process memo

### Process memo: preparing a message review

Inputs: a current brief, up to three owner-approved writing examples, claims
that need evidence, and the intended reader plus their decision.

1. List what the sources actually say before drafting.
2. Separate confirmed facts, preferences, and open questions.
3. Draft one clear argument for one reader.
4. Check every strong claim: source, gap, or remove it.
5. Ask the owner to change one thing before saving a rule.

Do not publish, send, promise scope, or invent proof. The workflow prepares a
review; an owner makes the decision.
